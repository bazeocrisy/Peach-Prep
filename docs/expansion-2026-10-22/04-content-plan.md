## 4. Content standards

### 4.1 Implementation constraint (for the lead and the UX agent)

`js/skills.js` splits `practice` at `ceil(n/2)`. Appending items to `practice` would silently move existing items from Try into Together, and it would change what the saved sets `SK:<key>:p` and `SK:<key>:y` mean. **The fifteen core items must live in a new field**, for example `core: [[5 ids/items], [5], [5]]` with `similar: [...]`, under a new, versioned progress key.
- Reused Try items keep their IDs and are referenced from `core`.
- `practice` stays unchanged, so Together keeps its current items.
- Choice order is shuffled once per sitting (`js/engine/items.js`). Resume must save that order, or re-derive it deterministically.

### 4.2 Set architecture

- Three sets of five. Each set stands alone, because the child may stop after any set.
- **Set 1, near transfer:** the lesson's existing practice passage or draft. Reused Try items go here first.
- **Set 2, new context:** a new short text in a different context domain.
- **Set 3, transfer and stretch:** a third context, a short fresh stem for transfer, and most of the DOK 3 items.
- Each set touches at least three of the lesson's components, or all of them if the lesson has three or fewer.

### 4.3 Passage reuse rules

1. New items **may** use the lesson's existing practice passages. No passage carries more than **seven** of the fifteen. In practice that means set 1 plus up to two items in later sets.
2. Every lesson with passages uses at least **three** different texts across the fifteen. A text can be an existing passage, a new passage of 100 to 250 words, a draft of six to 10 numbered sentences, or a short stem.
3. Across the fifteen, use at least **four** of the seven context domains: school, family, community, science, nature, sports, everyday problem solving. No single domain may cover more than six items.
4. **Never** reuse, paraphrase or share the key fact of any of these:
   - a check passage (form A or form B)
   - a Watch Me example
   - a mixed-set or Practice Test passage
   - another lesson's practice or check passage
   
   PP-004 found two such leaks (Ltexts-11 alligators, Ltexts-15 hummingbirds). Run the n-gram check in §8 on every batch.
5. Paired-text lessons (3.T.T.1.d, 3.T.T.2.c, 3.T.RA.1.b, 3.T.RA.1.c) need a new **pair** for set 2 and another for set 3. 3.T.RA.2.b needs short excerpt pairs, because practice has none today.
6. **Writing and GUM:** at least five of the fifteen items edit inside a numbered draft, the Milestones format. The rest may use single sentences, and no two sentences may share a frame.
7. Passages follow SPEC §6 and §5e:
   - original text
   - about 450 to 800L
   - figures only when the item is about the figure
   - Georgia settings welcome
   - no brands and no real private people
   - children in illustrations are Black by default
8. Judge difficulty in grade 3 terms, not by length alone. A short sentence can carry a demanding skill. Do not lengthen sentences to look harder.

### 4.4 Item variety (per fifteen)

| Task verb | Minimum | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Identify | at most 5 | name the trait, choose the linking word |
| Explain | 2 | why the author included a paragraph; why a word fits |
| Use evidence | 2 for reading lessons | EBSR Part B, hot text, "which detail best supports" |
| Edit | 2 (5 for writing and GUM) | fix, combine, replace or remove in a draft |
| Construct | 1 | build the sentence with dropdowns, order the plan, choose and assemble parts |
| Compare | 1 | two characters, two sources, two sentences |
| Transfer | 2 | same skill, fresh short text in a new domain (set 3) |

**Formats:** at least four formats per fifteen; `mc` in at most six items; at least three technology-enhanced formats (ms, hottext, dropdown, match, order, ebsr). This mirrors the blueprint, where two-point technology-enhanced items are 24 of 60 points.

**DOK:** at most two DOK 1 items. Reading and writing lessons need at least two DOK 3 items; language lessons need at least one. Today only 17 of 222 Try items are DOK 3.

### 4.5 The three graduated hints

The data shape is the UX/engine agent's decision. Suggested: keep `clue` as hint 1 for compatibility, and add `hint2` and `hint3`. The rules:

1. **Hint 1, strategy.** Remind the child of the lesson's strategy in one or two sentences, using the lesson's own words (clue words, the "ask" question). It must not narrow the choices or point to a location.
2. **Hint 2, focus.** Point to the relevant evidence or the first useful step: which paragraph or sentence, which word to cover, which feature to read. It may say how many answers to pick. It must not name or quote the key.
3. **Hint 3, specific scaffold.** Do part of the thinking. Restate the evidence in plain words, rule out one tempting distractor with a reason, or complete one of two slots. The child still chooses. It must not state the answer, quote the key option, or leave only one option standing.
4. All three are item-specific. The engine's generic Help 2 (cross out two) may stay as an extra on-request tool, but it does not count as a hint level.
5. **Ladder audit**, done by a reviewer for every item:
   - a reader who sees hint 1 and hint 2 but not the choices cannot write down the key;
   - hint 3 alone does not make the key the only option left.

### 4.6 Show Me How (the "how" steps)

- The button is labelled as revealing the answer. It works at any time, without wrong tries first.
- **Steps:** two to four short numbered steps that model the thinking, such as find the evidence, apply the rule, check against the question. Then the answer in one sentence. Then one line naming the likely misconception, taken from the lesson's `coach.mixup` where one exists. The existing `why` stays for test review.
- **Constructed or open responses** (for example a write-a-reason box, if one is added) show a sample response and two or three criteria. They never claim a single exact answer.
- After a reveal, the item is marked "answer shown". It never counts as correct independently. The next action offered is a similar question or Watch Me.

**Worked example** (existing item SK-TT1c-P4, "Which TWO words best describe Marisol by the end of the story?"; key: honest, thoughtful):
- **Hint 1:** "A trait is what a character is like inside. Look at what Marisol thinks and what she chooses, not only how she feels or wishes for a moment."
- **Hint 2:** "Reread paragraph 5. Who is Marisol thinking about, and why does it bother her?"
- **Hint 3:** "Greedy fits only her first wish, not the end of the story. Ask what kind of person her thoughts in paragraph 5 show, and what kind of person her choice by the end shows."
- **Show me how (reveals the answer):**
  1. In paragraph 5, she thinks about Mr. Okafor working in the hot sun and coming up short. Caring how someone else will be affected shows she is thoughtful.
  2. By the end, she has not kept money that was not hers, and Mr. Okafor thanks her for being honest. That shows she is honest.
  3. Check the others: greedy fits only her first wish, and careless and shy have no evidence in the story.

  Answer: honest and thoughtful. Common mix-up: picking a feeling or wish from one moment (wanting to keep the money) instead of a trait shown by her choices.

**Why hint 3 looks like this** (B0 review fix, 2026-10-22). An earlier version of this example said "Pick one word that fits giving back what is not yours, and one that fits thinking about how someone else will feel". Those are the definitions of the two keys, so the hint gave the answer away. Hint 3 may rule out one tempting distractor with a reason (here, greedy), or restate the evidence. It then ends with a neutral question. It must never describe the key in other words, and it must never point at the evidence another item in the same set asks for: the earlier hint 2 sent the child to Marisol's action in paragraph 6, which was the key of another set 1 item.

### 4.7 Similar-question bank

- **Size:** recommend **three per lesson**, one per set, for 285 items in all.
  - Three allows one reveal-recovery per set in a sitting, which is the case the assignment describes.
  - More would add about 95 review loads for every extra item per lesson, with little learning gain.
  - When the bank is used up, the page says so and offers Watch Me.
- Each similar item mirrors the component and DOK of its set. It uses a text that no core or check item uses, and it is reviewed like a core item.
- It is tracked under its own ID (for example `SK-TT1c-S1`). It never overwrites the original assisted attempt, and it never counts toward the fifteen.
- Selection is deterministic: the first unused similar item of the set. There is no runtime generation.

## 6. Fifteen-item quality bar

The fifteen in a lesson are **distinct** only if every pair differs in at least **two** of these four ways:
- the text or sentence set
- the component
- the task verb (§4.4)
- the context domain

Changing a name, an animal or an object inside the same frame changes none of them.

1. No two items share a stem frame and the same key logic. Three "Which word rhymes with ___?" items are one item, not three.
2. Every component of the lesson appears at least twice across the fifteen (at least once if the lesson has more than seven components). The hardest component appears in every set.
3. Context, verb, format and DOK minimums as in §4.3 and §4.4.
4. Exactly one defensible answer. For `ms` items, each correct choice is clearly supported and each wrong one is clearly not (SPEC §6.3).
5. Distractors:
   - They are plausible misreadings of the same length and grammar as the key.
   - None is absurd, and the key is not the only long or detailed option.
   - The answer word is not echoed in the stem (the Ltexts-5 pattern).
   - At least one distractor reflects the lesson's named misconception.
6. Every item in a writing or GUM draft has exactly one error, or one clear best revision (SPEC §6.6).
7. No leakage between core, Together, Watch Me, similar items, form A, form B, mixed sets, the Practice Test or other lessons (§4.3 rule 4).
8. Hints and Show Me How pass the ladder audit (§4.5).
9. The content stays inside grade 3. Nothing comes from a GUM row marked NYI for grade 3. Rules match what the lesson teaches: no new rule appears first in an item.
10. Each set stands alone, and the sets ramp from near (set 1) to transfer (set 3).
11. Child-facing directions stay as they are now ("Choose TWO"). Counts below ten are spelled out in prose. Numerals stay for codes, scores and points.
12. **Reused items** must meet the same bar. A reused Try item that collides with a new item is revised, not kept twice.

## 8. Independent review plan

**People.** Every batch has two reviewers. Neither wrote any item in that batch, and neither has read that batch's author log before solving. PP-004 lost blindness when reviewers read the fix log first ("A2 had read the afix log first"), so the order is enforced.

**Reviewer A, blind solver**
1. Gets a keyless, shuffled dump of the batch: stems, passages and choices only, with no clue, hints, why, how or `correct` flags.
2. Solves every core and similar item and records answers with a confidence mark.
3. Only then compares against the keys. Every disagreement is a finding, either a wrong key or ambiguity.
4. Then reads the hint ladder and Show Me How for each item and runs the ladder audit (§4.5).

**Reviewer B, alignment and quality.** Works independently of A, with the matrix (`02`) and the quality bar (§6). Checks:
- that each item hits the component it claims, and that the lesson's fifteen cover the components;
- distinctness, using the two-of-four rule;
- distractor plausibility;
- grade suitability and readability, with `verification/readability.py`;
- leakage, with an n-gram similarity scan of each new item against every Watch Me, Together, form A, form B, mixed-set, Practice Test and other-lesson item. Any match of six or more words, or the same key fact, is flagged.

**Severity.**
- **MUST:** a wrong or ambiguous key, a hint that reveals the answer, misalignment, leakage, or a rule error.
- **SHOULD:** a weak distractor, a repeated frame, or an off-balance mix.

A batch is accepted with zero open MUST findings. The author fixes. A reviewer who has not seen the fix log re-solves every changed item blind.

**Gates for every batch:**
