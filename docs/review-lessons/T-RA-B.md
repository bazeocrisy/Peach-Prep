## MUST
- [SK-RA2a-P2] The clue gives away Part B. It says "Paragraph 3 tells how fireflies use their light", but only one Part B choice comes from paragraph 3. It then says "choose the sentence that shows two fireflies answering each other", which repeats the key's own word ("flashes back to **answer** him"). ## SHOULD
- [SK-RA2a-P1] The clue "Look for a sentence that tells about heat" nearly gives the answer away, because p1:2:3 is the only target that mentions heat. Fix: change it to "The question asks about how the light feels, not where it comes from or what it is used for. Which sentence tells that?"
- [SK-RA2a-T3] Target p2:4:2 ("She thought about all those afternoons at the piano...") can be defended as showing Ana becoming "sure of herself", because remembering her practice is what gives her confidence. An adult could argue for it. Fix: replace that target with p2:1:3 ("In ten minutes, she would play the piano in front of the whole school."), which is clearly not about calm.
- [SK-RA2a-T4] The clue ("The story already shows she does well. Find the sentence that shows the hard work.") leaves only one choice about work, so it does most of the item for the child. This is less serious on Test, where there are no hints, but the clue is still stored. Fix: change it to "Leo's opinion is about what Ana did to get ready. Which sentence shows that?" or make it more general.
- [SK-RA1b-P2] The clue and the key conflict. The clue says "The fact must tell something about bees", but the key ("Only flowers that get pollen can grow into pumpkins.") never mentions bees, and the child has to infer the link. A child who follows the clue literally will find no choice that fits. Fix: make the key "Without bees, Ms. Ruiz would get very few pumpkins." (from Source 2, paragraph 2), or change the clue to "Think about what bees carry. Which fact shows why that matters to pumpkins?"
- [SK-RA1b-T1/T3/T4/T5] Four of the five test items depend on the same fact that the moon has no air and astronauts wore space suits. T1 has the row "The moon has no air" (Both), T4's key is "The moon has no air" (Both), T3 has the space-suits key, and T5's key is the space-suits sentence. So T1 effectively answers T4, and T3 answers T5. Fix: change T4 to ask which fact is in both sources using "no wind or rain", or move "no wind or rain" out of T1. Also give T5 a different link between the sources, for example a Source 1 note "The moon has no wind or rain" paired with the key "Mr. Okafor said the footprints are probably still there."
- [SK-RA1a-T4] This item is a near-clone of P3: same layout, same 2×2 pattern, the same "Are X scarier/cooler than Y?" opinion row and the same "Do I like/want..." row. It tests whether the child remembers P3, not whether they have mastered the skill. Fix: change the wrong rows to a different kind, such as a yes-or-no fact the child already knows ("Do owls have wings?") or an off-topic question ("What do eagles eat?"), so the child has to apply the definition in a new way.
- [SK-RA1a-P1] Two of the three distractors are too obviously wrong ("Do I like honey better than jam?" and "How many jars of honey are in my kitchen?"). The key also repeats the prompt's wording ("how ... make honey"). Fix: replace one distractor with a plausible on-topic but weaker question, such as "Where do honeybees live?" or "Do bees make honey?" (yes-or-no).
- [3.T.RA.1.c Teach, points[4]] The point "Use linking words like also, in addition and in the fall" calls "in the fall" a linking word. It is a time phrase. The point is misleading as written, and P4 and T5 depend on it. Fix: change it to "Use linking words like also and in addition, or time words like in the fall, to join ideas from both sources."
- [3.T.RA.1.c Learn 1] Step 4 tells the child to join the ideas "with a linking word like also", but the stated answer uses "and" and never uses also. The steps and the answer do not match. Fix: change the answer to "Emperor penguins live in a very cold place where thick feathers and fat keep them warm. They also huddle together in winter to stay warm." Or change step 4 to "Join the ideas in one sentence with a linking word like and."

## NIT
- [3.T.RA.1.a Learn 2] The text says squirrels "use their strong sense of smell to find the nuts". Smell is real, but research shows they also rely on memory. This is acceptable for grade 3. If you want it fully accurate, write "They use their memory and strong sense of smell."
- [3.T.RA.1.b p1, paragraph 4] "A tiny green pumpkin forms where the flower was." The tiny pumpkin is actually already at the base of the female flower before pollination, and pollination makes it grow. Possible rewrite: "A tiny green pumpkin at the bottom of the flower starts to grow."
- [3.T.RA.1.c Teach example] "Tiny green plants called algae" is a common simplification. Green algae are only loosely "plants". "Tiny green living things called algae" is safer.
- [SK-RA1c-T1] In the key, "nest here in summer" leaves "here" unclear. Change it to "nest in Georgia in summer".
- [SK-RA1c-T3] In the key, "In winter, they fly south" does not match the source, which says they leave in late summer and fly south *for* the winter. Change it to "They fly south for the winter and come back in spring."
- [SK-RA1c-T4] In option 4, "in fall they fly south" does not match "In late summer..." in Source 2. A very careful child might hesitate over it. Change it to "and in late summer they fly south."
- [SK-RA1c-T2] The distractor "They spend the summer in Georgia and the eastern United States" adds "eastern United States", which is not in Source 2. Change it to "They come to Georgia in spring and stay all summer."
- [SK-RA2b-P4] The prompt says "grown in Georgia", but the second key (the fruit farmer's book) is not tied to Georgia. Change it to "a library book by a Georgia fruit farmer about caring for peach trees", or drop "in Georgia" from the prompt.
- [SK-RA2b-T3] The row "a science center book about how birds build nests" reuses the off-topic example from Teach (a nature center page about how birds build nests). Use a different off-topic subject, for example "how snakes shed their skin".
- [SK-RA2b-P2] This item is nearly the same as Learn 2: same topic (tornadoes), and the same key and distractor wording. That is acceptable for Practice, but a different topic would make the child apply the skill rather than recognize it.
