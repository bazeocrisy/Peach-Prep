## MUST
- [3.F.F.1.c / check item 1] "Read at about the typical speed for this time of year, or better" blocks mastery of a standard that does not name rate. The official text is "appropriate prosody (phrasing, expression, pauses, intonation)". Because the goal is the 50th percentile, about half of typical third graders cannot check this line by definition. The check intro also says to use "a passage they have practiced". Hasbrouck and Tindal norms are for cold reads of unpracticed text, so comparing a practiced read against them is not a valid measure. In short, the gate tests something outside the named standard, and measures it wrongly. Fix: remove the rate line. If you want a pace line, make it prosody-based: "Read at a talking pace, not rushed and not word by word."

## SHOULD
- [3.F.F.1.b / check item 2] "Fixed at least one mistake by rereading, without being told" cannot be checked by a child who reads with no errors, so an accurate reader is blocked from mastery. Self-correction is also its own standard (3.F.F.1.d). Fix: "If they misread a word, they noticed and fixed it (check this if there were no mistakes)." Or drop the line.
- [3.F.F.1.b / check] The standard says "a variety of unfamiliar grade-level texts with increasing accuracy". The checklist is one snapshot on one passage. Fix: change item 1 to "Read two new passages of different kinds (a story and a fact text) at about 95% or better." The timer already keeps a history, so you could add "Accuracy stayed the same or went up across readings."
- [shared FLUENCY.how vs 3.F.F.1.c] The activity says "Pick a passage the reader has not practiced". The 1.c check intro and parent note say to use a practiced passage and repeat it. The grown-up gets opposite instructions on the same screen. Fix: give the 1.c lesson its own `how`, for example "For expression, practice one passage a few times, then read it for the grown-up."
- [3.F.F.1.b / teach.tip] "The Milestones does not time reading" can be read as "the test is untimed". Milestones sections do have time limits, and the 1.c tip says smooth reading "saves time" on it. The two tips contradict each other. Fix: "The Milestones does not measure how fast you read out loud, but every question depends on reading the passage accurately."
- [3.F.F.1.c / teach.points[1] and check item 3] "Let your voice go up at a question mark" and "Voice went up for questions" are only true for yes/no questions. Wh-questions ("Where is my shoe?") naturally fall, so a child reading them correctly would fail the check line. Fix the point: "At a question mark, make it sound like asking; yes/no questions usually go up at the end." Fix the check line: "Made questions sound like questions and showed feeling for exclamations."
- [3.F.F.1.c / learn[0]] The text shown already contains the slashes ("After lunch / the class walked / to the library."), so the answer to "Where should the reader group the words?" is visible before step 1. Fix: show the sentence without slashes and keep the slashes in the answer only.
- [3.F.F.1.c / check item 5] "Used a different voice for characters who were speaking" cannot be shown on passage 2 (bees), which has no dialogue. Fix: start the intro with "Use passage 1 or 3, which have characters talking," or reword the line as "...if the passage has talking."
- [3.F.H.3.a / learn[1]] The steps for "Sam swims" only say where the s's are ("Find the capital S", "it begins with a lowercase s"). They never model how to recognize the tricky shape, so they restate the answer rather than teach a strategy. Capital S was also not on the teach list of tricky letters. Fix: add a shape step, for example "A cursive s swings up to a point, then curves down and closes on the bottom line, like a little sail," and add S to the tricky capitals or reword the ask.
- [SK-FH3a-P5] The distractors "Lou" and "Gus" share almost nothing with the cursive Z-o-e (wrong first letter shape, wrong vowels and ending), so they are too obviously wrong. Fix: keep "Joe" and replace the other two with names that share the "-oe" ending or start with Z, for example "Moe" and "Zack".
- [3.F.H.4.a / teach.points[1]] "e, l, h, k, b and f start with a loop" is wrong for traditional US cursive. These letters start with the same up-stroke (undercurve) as i, t, u and w, and the loop forms at the top of that up-stroke. A child told to "start with a loop" will begin the letter wrong. Fix: "i, t, u and w start with an up-stroke. e, l, h, k, b and f start with the same up-stroke, then loop back down."
- [3.F.H.4.a / teach.points] The families taught leave out two rows that the tracing sheet uses: "r s p j" (up-stroke starts) and "m n v x y z" (these start with an over-curve, a hump stroke). The child traces these rows with no stroke guidance. Fix: add a point: "r, s, p and j also start with the up-stroke. m, n, v, x, y and z start with a hump, an over-curve."
- [3.F.H.4.a / teach] The standard is "forming cursive letters and connectors within words". The hardest connectors in traditional cursive are the four letters that end high, b, o, v and w (bridge or checkstroke joins), and they are not taught. The trace words "bike", "zoo" and "dog" require them. Learn #2 mentions the o ending at the top only in passing. Fix: add a point: "b, o, v and w end with a little swing at the middle line, so the next letter starts from up there, not from the bottom line." Add one Learn example on "ow" or "be".
- [3.F.H.4.a / teach.points[3] and check item 4] In traditional US cursive (the Zaner-Bloser style that Playwrite US Trad follows), lowercase f and z also go below the bottom line. The check line "g, j, p, q and y hang below the bottom line" reads as the full list and leaves out f and z. Fix: in the point, write "g, j, p, q, y and z hang below, and f goes both above and below." Make the check line match.
- [3.F.H.4.a / check item 5] "Writes their own first and last name in cursive with capital letters" gates mastery on capitals that are never taught or traced. Every Teach point, Learn example and trace row is lowercase. Fix: add a capitals row to the tracing sheet and a teach point on capitals (including that some do not join), or change the line to lowercase only ("Writes their first name in cursive").
- [3.F.H.4.a / learn[1]] "The loop swings back up to the bottom line and keeps going into the o" is inaccurate. The o begins at the middle line, so the g's exit stroke has to rise past the bottom line up to the middle line. A child following the step will start the o on the bottom line. Fix: "The loop crosses at the bottom line and keeps swinging up to the middle line, where the o begins."
- [3.F.H.4.b / teach.points[3] and check item 1] "Letters inside a word should be close and joined" and "Every word is joined inside, with no gaps" conflict with traditional cursive. Several capitals (for example T, W, O, P and V in Zaner-Bloser) usually do not join the next letter. The trace rows "The sun is hot." and "We read a book." start with such capitals, so a child writing them correctly would fail the line. Fix: add "(some capital letters, like T and W, do not join the next letter)" to both.
- [3.F.H.4.b / learn] Both worked examples are shown in print, not cursive, in a lesson about cursive spacing. In "sun   ny day" the triple space collapses to one space in HTML, so the "big gap" looks like an ordinary word space. Fix: use the "Cursive:" prefix (e.g. "Cursive: thebigtree") and render the gap with `   ` or describe it in words.
