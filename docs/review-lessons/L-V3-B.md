## SHOULD
- [3.L.V.3.a Teach / scope] The official standard says "including images and graphs" and "words and phrases." Teach covers only words found in sentences. Nothing covers using a picture, chart or caption, or working out a phrase. The site shows no images, but a `Box:` with a simple table or a caption-style text box could carry this. Fix: add one Teach point ("Pictures, charts and captions can also give clues about a word") and, if possible, one item that uses a Box text box as the "beyond the sentence" source. At the least, add one multiword phrase item (for example, "gobbling up" or "picked up").
- [SK-V3a-P5 clue] "'which are' gives a meaning, 'such as' gives examples, and 'but' shows an opposite. If none of those, look for a word that means the same" maps each signal straight to a column, so all four rows are solved by spotting signal words. That is most of the item. Fix: "For each sentence, find the words that help you know the meaning. Do they tell the meaning, give examples, mean the same thing or mean the opposite?"
- [SK-V3b-P2 clue] "Kofi says two things about the fish, one right after the other" points to paragraph 5, and only one Part B choice comes from paragraph 5. It is less blunt than the MUST cases but still all but names the key. Fix: "Look at the words near enormous. Is there a word that means almost the same thing?"
- [SK-V3b-P3] This lesson is 3.L.V.3.b (homophones and homographs), but the prompt frames scales as "has more than one meaning," which is the 3.L.V.3.a multiple-meaning skill. Scales does fit the lesson's own homograph definition, so call it one to tie the item to this standard. Fix: "Scales is a homograph. What does scales mean in paragraph 4?" and add one line to the why: "All four meanings are spelled scales, so it is a homograph."
- [3.L.V.3.d passage p1, sentence 3:2 and glossary] Calling the chrysalis "the hard case a caterpillar makes" is misleading. A chrysalis is not built or spun like a cocoon. It is the caterpillar's own new outer skin, which hardens after the caterpillar sheds its last skin. "Makes" invites the common chrysalis/cocoon mix-up. Fix: glossary "chrysalis (noun): the hard outer shell a caterpillar's body forms while it turns into a butterfly." The passage sentence "forms a chrysalis" is fine as written.
- [SK-V3d-P5, SK-V3d-T5 / standard alignment] Both "check spelling" items are spelling recognition with "dictionary" in the prompt. The child never uses a dictionary feature such as ABC order, guide words or an entry. Fix (one of them is enough): make it a guide-word spelling check, for example "Leo looks for because on the page with guide words beach – bell. Which spelling will he find there?", so the child has to place the correct spelling between the guide words. Or show a short entry and ask which spelling matches it.
- [SK-V3e-P3] The prompt hands the child the answer type ("like shouted, to show HOW someone spoke"), and sentence 6 is the only target where anyone speaks. The item is DOK 1 at best and adds nothing beyond Learn 1, which uses the same said-to-shouted change. Fix: drop "like shouted, to show HOW someone spoke" from the prompt ("In one sentence, Malik used a plain verb even though the sentence tells exactly how it was done. Choose that sentence."), or retarget it to sentence 8 or 9 (nice, fun).
- [SK-V3e-T1] The key stands out. Gentle, quiet and soft all mean "calm," so the one strong word is the obvious answer even for a child who never reads sentence 4. Fix: use near-miss distractors of the same strength type, for example "fierce" (key), "chilly", "noisy", "steady", so the child has to match the detail "big storm" and not just pick the only strong word.
- [SK-V3c-P4 slot 2] "boiling" and "freezing" for water that is "just right for a bath" are too obviously wrong. The item then tests nothing about shades of meaning in that slot. Fix: options "cool", "warm", "hot" (key warm), so the child has to tell apart close words on the ladder.
- [SK-V3c-P1 clue] "Which word means it is about to turn to ice?" names the last step (freezing) through its meaning. In a 3-step order item that leaves only one swap to decide. Fix: "Picture a glass of water getting colder and colder. Which word comes first, and which comes last?"

## NIT
- [SK-V3a-P4 why] "The other sentences use ... a clue in the next sentence." p1:3:2 ("Only two people stopped by...") is the clue sentence itself, not a sentence that has a word with a clue in the next sentence. Fix: "...an example clue, a sentence that is itself a clue for scarce, and a synonym clue."
- [3.L.V.3.c Teach point 4 / Learn 2] "glad is a little pleased, happy is more" ranks two near-equal words. Most adults would not defend glad < happy. No item depends on it, since only the strongest word (thrilled) is tested. Fix: "Happy and glad both mean pleased. Thrilled is much stronger."
- [SK-V3c-P3] DOK 3 is generous for a recall-and-locate item. Fix: dok 2.
- [SK-V3e-T4 why/clue] "because they were worried after the storm" and "Sofia's family is worried" state a feeling that the draft never says. It is a fair inference, but say it as one. Fix: "After a big storm, the family would want to check the garden fast."
- [SK-V3e-P1] "A whale shark glided slowly past the glass" is a little redundant, because glide already means smooth and slow. It is fine for grade 3, but the why could note that glided shows "smoothly." No change needed beyond the why.
- [3.L.V.3.d Teach] The standard says "print or digital." Teach never mentions an online dictionary. Fix: add to point 5: "You can use a dictionary in a book or online."
- [SK-V3d-T5] A beginning dictionary usually lists finish, with -ed shown under it, so "finished" may not be its own entry. Fix: in the prompt, say "Pablo looks up finish in a dictionary to check how to spell finished."
- [SK-V3b-P5 / SK-V3b-T4, SK-V3c-P1 / SK-V3c-T1] Each test item uses the same format and prompt as its practice twin. The words differ, so it is acceptable, but swapping one test item to another format (for example, a dropdown) would better show mastery.
- [3.L.V.3.b passage p2, paragraph 2] "When vinegar is poured in, baking soda inside makes foam" describes the plan, and then the story reveals that the baking soda was forgotten. That works as a twist, but a careful child may see a contradiction. Fix: "When vinegar is poured onto baking soda inside, foam bubbles up and spills over the top."
