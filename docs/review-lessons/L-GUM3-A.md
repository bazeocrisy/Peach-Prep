## MUST
- [SK-GUM3-1-P4] "The frog's jumped into the pond." is grammatical when frog's is read as "the frog has": "The frog has jumped into the pond." Teach point 5 tells the child that 's can stand for "has" (it's = it has). So an adult can defend that this sentence has no error, and the item then has no error to select. Fix: make the plural unmistakable, for example "Two frog's jumped into the pond.", as SK-GUM3-1-T4 already does with "Two bird's sat on the fence."
- [SK-GUM3-2-P5] The clue gives the answer away. "Neither word adds -s" rules out childs and childrens in slot 0 and mouses and mices in slot 1, so each slot has only one option left: children and mice. Fix: change the clue to something like "Both blanks need a word for more than one. Child and mouse are special nouns. Say 'one ___, two ___.'"
- [SK-GUM3-8-T1] The key (basket) matches the dictionary, but under the lesson's only rule ("split between the beats you hear when you clap it") a careful adult can defend honey. Spoken honey sounds like "hun-ee," and the n is heard at the start of the second beat, so "ho- | ney" falls between the beats as heard. "mark- | et" versus "mar- | ket" also cannot be told apart by clapping. Telling them apart takes the spelling rules for dividing words (split between two consonants: bas-ket, mar-ket; keep the consonant with a short vowel: hon-ey). Teach never states those rules. Fix: either add a Teach point on those rules with a Learn example, or replace the distractors with breaks that clapping clearly shows are wrong (for example "marke- | t" and "hone- | y"). Also give the item a fourth choice (see SHOULD).
- [SK-GUM3-8-T2] The same problem. Spoken butterfly is "buh-ter-fly," so a careful adult following the lesson's clap rule can defend "bu- | tterfly" as a break between beats. Only the doubled-consonant convention (but-ter) makes it wrong, and that convention is never taught. Fix: add a Teach point, "When a word has a double letter in the middle, split between the two letters: but-ter, rab-bit, kit-ten," or replace "bu- | tterfly" with a break that clapping shows is wrong, for example "butte- | rfly".

## SHOULD
- [SK-GUM3-4-T5] [SK-GUM3-8-T1] These mc items have 3 choices, but SPEC §5 says mc has 4. The checker does not catch this. Fix: add a fourth choice. For 4-T5, "yet" works; do not use "and", because "The puppy was very tired, and it took a long nap." is defensible. For 8-T1, add a fourth split word to the stem.
- [Lesson 8 Teach] The lesson teaches only "split between the beats you hear." That is not enough to answer T1 and T2, and it does not explain the P3 and P4 distractors (gard- | en). Fix: add one point: "Look at the letters too: split between two consonants (bas-ket, gar-den) or between double letters (rab-bit)."
- [Lesson 8 Test vs Practice] The test reuses Practice and Learn words. SK-GUM3-8-T1's key, basket, has the same split as the key "bas- | ket" in SK-GUM3-8-P4. Turtle (Learn, then T3) and garden (Teach and P4) also come back. A child can score by memory. Fix: use new words in the test, for example "pic- | nic", "lad- | der" and "sil- | ver".
- [SK-GUM3-6-T4] [SK-GUM3-6-P1] Teach defines a reflexive pronoun only as "the person doing the action is also the one it happens to." The keys "I washed all of the dishes by myself." and "The kids cleaned up the room themselves." (and "swung shut by itself" in P1) are the "by ___self" and emphatic uses, which that definition does not cover. A child applying the Teach rule could reject a correct key. Fix: add a Teach point: "You can also use a -self word to mean 'alone' or 'with no help': I did it by myself. They cleaned it themselves."
- [SK-GUM3-7-P4] The prompt says "use commas correctly," but one key, "Yusuf can sing and play the guitar.", has no comma. That confuses a child who looks for a comma. Fix: "Choose the TWO sentences that are punctuated correctly."
- [SK-GUM3-4-P3] The clue "Which word from that list [FANBOYS] is in the sentence?" picks out the one choice on the list (or), so it nearly gives the answer away. Fix: "Which word joins two choices of what to do?"
- [Lesson 3 Teach, point 5] The list of past-tense time words includes "this morning," which also goes with present and future ("I will read this morning"). Fix: replace it with "last night".
