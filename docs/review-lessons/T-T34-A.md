## MUST
- [SK-TT4b-P1] There are two defensible answers. Stanza 3's first couplet is cold / red. The couplet fails to rhyme, but nothing makes line 2 the one at fault rather than line 1. An adult could pick "The sun sinks low, the air is cold," (p1:3:1), because changing line 1 to end in a word like "bed" or "red" would repair the pair just as well. Both lines are targets. Fix: remove "p1:3:1" from `targets`. (Optionally reword the prompt to "Line 1 of stanza 3 ends with cold. Which line breaks Jaylen's rhyme pattern?")
- [SK-TT4b-T1] There are two defensible answers, for the same reason. Stanza 2 pairs line 2 "blue" with line 4 "away", and both lines are targets. "Its wings a shiny blue." (p2:2:2) is just as defensible as the line that breaks the pattern, because changing it to "Its wings a shiny gray." would make lines 2 and 4 rhyme and keep the meaning. Fix: remove "p2:2:2" from `targets` (keep 2:1 and 2:3 as distractors), or reword the prompt so line 2 is fixed ("Line 2 of stanza 2 ends with blue. Which line ...").

## SHOULD
- [SK-TT4b-T2] The key "But misses! Off it flew." shifts tense inside the line ("misses" is present, "flew" is past). The rest of the poem is in present tense: sits, zips, shoots. A grade 3 model answer should not show a verb-tense shift, since tense consistency is taught in the GUM rows. Fix: use a present-tense line that rhymes with blue, for example "It zips right out of view." Update `why` to match ("View rhymes with blue, and it keeps Rosa's meaning that the dragonfly gets away").
- [SK-TT3c-P3 why] "But shows something different, or gives a choice, and until tells time." Without quotation marks, a child reads this as saying "but" both shows something different and gives a choice. That is a misleading claim about linking words. Fix: "\"But\" shows something different, \"or\" gives a choice, and \"until\" tells time."
- [SK-TT4a-P2 clue] The clue lists the four end words ("rain, yard, up, hard") and asks which two sound alike. For a DOK 1 rhyme item, that leaves almost nothing for the child to do; the clue is close to the answer. Fix: "Read only the last word of each line in stanza 2 out loud. Listen for two that end with the same sound." Do not list the words.

## NIT
- [3.T.T.3.a / p2 + SK-TT3a-T5] Paragraph 1 says the playground "does not have a single tree." The key then mentions "the tree by our parking lot." The two statements are consistent, since the parking lot is not the playground. Still, a careful child may reject the key because "the school has no trees." Consider "a robin built a nest in a tree across the street from our school."
- [3.T.T.3.c teach.points[2]] "Because joins a reason to the why behind it" is circular, because a reason already is the why. Consider: "Because joins a reason to the fact or example that explains it."
- [3.T.T.3.c teach.example] Point 4 says a conclusion should "say your opinion again and sum up your reasons." The example's conclusion only restates the opinion. Consider: "Therefore, because cooking teaches math and brings families together, every family should invite kids into the kitchen."
- [SK-TT3c-T5] The item is marked dok 3. Picking which numbered sentences are evidence is DOK 2. Fix: dok 2.
- [3.T.T.4.b teach.examples[1]] "My boots go thumping up the stair." The singular "stair" reads oddly for "stairs". It is acceptable as poetic license, so this is optional.
