## MUST
- [SK-SS1a-P2] The item has more than one defensible answer. The prompt "Select the sentence that tells an effect" is too loose. Sentence 2, "Their roots work with tiny living things to add plant food to the soil," also tells an effect: adding plant food is the result of the roots working with tiny living things. An adult could defend it. Sentence 1, "Peanut plants are good for the soil," can also be read as an effect of the plants on the soil. The clue ("look for a clue word that signals a reason") then points straight at the only sentence that contains "Because". Fix: narrow the prompt to one cause, for example "Select the sentence that tells how peanut plants affect the crops planted in that field the next year." Change the clue to "First find the cause the section talks about. Then find the sentence that tells what happens later because of it."

## SHOULD
- [SK-SS1a-T2 Part B] The prompt says "Which sentence from that section," but two of the four choices are not in "A Home for Many Animals". "The gopher tortoise is Georgia's state reptile." is in the intro, and "Some land is now kept safe so tortoises can live there." is in "Working Together". A child who checks the section rules them out at once, so the item is really a two-choice item. Fix: use only sentences from that section, for example "A gopher tortoise uses its strong front legs to dig a long tunnel in the sand." and "A burrow stays cool on hot days and warmer on cold nights." The second one is a strong distractor because it states the cause.
- [SK-SS1a-P1] The distractor "It tells what causes peanuts to taste salty." is too obviously wrong, because nothing in the passage is about taste. Fix: use something the text touches on, for example "It tells why peanut plants are good for the soil."
- [3.T.SS.1.a Teach / SK-SS1a-P4] The match row "the trouble was, to fix it" tests clue words for problem and solution, but Teach gives no clue words for that pattern. It only says "Problem and solution tells about a trouble and how it gets fixed." Fix: add "Look for problem, trouble, solve and fix" to that teaching point. Also consider splitting the fourth point, which teaches two patterns in one bullet.
- [3.T.SS.1.a passage p2, "A Big Problem"] "Long ago, gopher tortoises lived in large pine forests in south Georgia" suggests they no longer live there. In fact they still live in south Georgia's longleaf pine forests, and the next section says land is kept safe for them. Fix: "Long ago, large pine forests covered much of south Georgia, and many gopher tortoises lived there." This keeps p2:5:1 as sentence 1, so no refs change.
- [SK-SS1b-T2] The key stands out. "Fun Events for Kids" repeats the words of sentence 6, "The library also has fun events for kids." A child can match words without judging the section. Fix: rephrase the key, for example "Things to Do at the Library". Or reword sentence 6 so it does not contain the exact heading.
- [SK-SS1d-T5] The key stands out. The prompt says the new paragraph is "about the awards", and the key is the only choice that mentions awards. Fix: drop "about the awards" from the prompt (for example, "Carlos wants to add a new paragraph to tell what happened at the end of the day"). Or make at least one distractor mention awards without being a topic sentence, for example "My friend Eli won a blue ribbon at the awards."
- [3.T.SS.1.c Test] The test mirrors Practice item for item. T1 matches P1 (dropdown: "join sentences 2 and 3", reason word). T2 matches P3 (add For example; the three distractors are fragments). T3 matches P2 ("join sentences 8 and 9" with Although/Because/Since/For example). The passages differ, but the frames are the same, so the test checks memory of the format. Fix: change at least two test items to a different frame. For example, T3 could be "Which word best begins sentence 9?" with However-free options such as Also, For example and Although, rewritten to fit. Or turn T2 into an ms or hottext item.
- [SK-SS1c-T2] All three distractors are sentence fragments (Although… / Because… / Since…). A child can pick the key by spotting the only complete sentence, without knowing what "for example" does. Practice item P3 uses the same trick. Fix: give complete-sentence distractors that use the wrong linking word, for example "Also, apples, blueberries and peaches grow from flowers that bees visit." That one is weak but complete, so check that it cannot be defended. Other options: "Because of this, apples…", or "Although they are sweet, apples, blueberries and peaches grow from flowers that bees visit."
- [SK-SS1d-T2 / SK-SS1d-T3] Two test items depend on the same judgment: the paragraph starting at sentence 7 is about water games after lunch. T2's `why` states "That paragraph is about water games after lunch," which is the answer to T3 Part A, if the `why` is shown after a correct answer. T3 Part B then reduces to "pick the first sentence". Fix: point T3 at the races paragraph, or at the opening, or replace T3 with a different skill, such as choosing the best place to start a new paragraph.
- [SK-SS2a-P3] The clue "Look for a clue that tells how Rosa feels just before Ms. Ellis speaks" points to the exact Part B sentence ("…saw Rosa's worried face"), and the word "worried" there gives away Part A. Fix: "There are no real butterflies at the pool. Look in paragraph 2 for words that tell how Rosa looks or feels."
