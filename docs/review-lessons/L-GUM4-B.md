I solved all 80 Practice and Test items before checking the keys. Every key matched my answer, and no item has a second defensible answer. Every Learn example's steps reach the stated answer. Each lesson teaches its official `gum` row at grade-3 scope. The one exception is the relative-pronoun lesson, which has a false rule (see MUST).

## MUST
- [3.L.GC.1:I:use-relative-pronouns-and-relative-adverbs / Teach tip, Teach point 3, Learn steps, SK-GUM4-5-P5] The tip says the noun before the blank "tells you which word to use": a place takes where and a time takes when. That grammar claim is false. A place noun takes that or which when the next part has no doer of its own: "We visited the farm that has goats." "Atlanta is a city that has many parks." The lesson itself breaks the rule. In SK-GUM4-5-T5, "The park who has a slide is near us" is fixed with **that**, not where ("The park where has a slide" is wrong). If a child follows the tip, they get the wrong fix. Its `why` ("A park and a store are places ... so those words do not fit") supports the faulty rule and never names the right word. SK-GUM4-5-P5 ("Which word would you use to tell more about each noun?" for "the library", "the kitchen", "the morning") drills the same false rule, because "the library that opened" is just as correct. Fix: (1) Rewrite the tip as "Look at the noun before the blank. For a person, use who. For a thing, use that or which. For a place or time, use where or when when you could say 'in that place' or 'at that time': the park where we fly kites (we fly kites in that place)." (2) Add a Teach clause: "If the next words need someone or something to do the action, use that: the park that has a slide." (3) Reword P5 to sentence frames so a single word fits, e.g. "the library ___ we borrow books", "the morning ___ it snowed". (4) In T5's `why`, say the park sentence should use that, the store sentence should use where, and the Liam sentence should use who.

## SHOULD
- [3.L.GC.1:I:use-relative-pronouns-and-relative-adverbs / item coverage] The standard names the relative pronouns who, which and that, but no Practice or Test item makes that or which the answer. Every keyed answer is who, where or when. Only Learn 2 models that. Fix: replace one Practice and one Test dropdown with a thing noun keyed to that or which, e.g. "Mom fixed the lamp {0} was broken." [that / who / where / when].
- [3.L.GC.1:C:form-and-use-comparative-and-superlative-adjecti / Teach points] Teach splits words only into "short words add -er/-est" and "many longer words use more/most". The items, though, rely on rules Teach never states: two-syllable -y words take -er/-est (quieter, happiest, funniest in P4, T2), y changes to i (funniest, happiest), and some words double the last consonant (bigger in P1). The `why`s mention these rules after the fact. A child who counts "happy" or "funny" as a longer word could reason toward "more funny". Fix: add one point: "Words that end in y, like happy and funny, change y to i and add -er or -est: happier, funniest. Some short words double the last letter: big, bigger, biggest."
- [3.L.GC.1:C:ensure-pronoun-antecedent-agreement / Teach example] "The twins packed their bags, and Mom carried them to the car." Them could mean the twins or the bags. The note says bags, but a lesson on finding the antecedent should not model an unclear one. Fix: "The twins packed their bags and zipped them shut."
- [SK-GUM4-3-P3] The distractor "the science fair" does not appear in the stem sentence, so it is too obviously wrong. The `why` also says the project is wrong because "a project cannot own anything". The lesson's actual reason is that their means more than one and project means one. Fix: replace the distractor with a noun from the sentence, e.g. "the project and Mr. Hill", or rewrite the stem to add a second plural noun ("The girls showed their science project to the judges."). Change the `why` to the one-or-more-than-one reason.
- [SK-GUM4-4-T5] The Test reuses the Practice sentence "Supper is ready" from Grandma (SK-GUM4-4-P5, "Grandma said, Supper is ready."). Test sentences must differ from Practice. Fix: change the T5 sentence to a new one, e.g. "{0} said Uncle Dev." with options built on "The game starts at noon,".

## NIT
- [3.L.GC.1:I:use-helping-and-linking-verbs / Teach tip] The tip says "Look at the word after the verb". In "My uncle was a firefighter" and "Ms. Hall is our music teacher", the next word is a or our. Fix: "Look at the words after the verb."
- [SK-GUM4-7-T2] The `why` begins "The answer is is.", which is hard to read aloud. Fix: "The answer is the word is."
- [SK-GUM4-6-P4] Some dictionaries list "please" as an interjection. The clue's punctuation test still rules it out, but a cleaner distractor would remove any doubt. Fix: "We fed the ducks at the pond."
- [SK-GUM4-6-T5] "Oh, I forgot my jacket." is nearly the same as the Teach line "Oh, I forgot my pencil." Fix: "Oh, the soup is ready."
- [SK-GUM4-2-T3] "My cough is worse today than it was yesterday." is close to Learn 2 ("My cold was bad on Monday, but it was even ___ on Tuesday."). Fix: use a non-illness context, e.g. "The traffic is worse today than it was yesterday."
- [SK-GUM4-3-P2 `why`] "They ... is used before the action word" brings in subject and object pronouns, which this agreement lesson does not otherwise teach. It also uses "it" for "they", which is hard to follow. Fix: "They also means more than one, but we say 'ate them', not 'ate they'."
