I solved all 90 Practice and Test items before checking the keys. Every key matched my answer, and I found no item with a second defensible answer. All nine lessons match their official `gum` row and stay in grade 3 scope. Every Learn example reaches its stated answer.

## MUST
- [GUM2-7 Teach example note; also Learn 2 step 1] The note says "Each preposition comes right before a noun." That is false for its own example: in "under her bed", under comes right before her, not a noun. Learn 2 step 1 builds on the same idea ("Look for a small word that comes right before a noun"). A child using that strategy would pick "the" in "during the fire drill", because the also comes right before a noun. Fix: change the note to "Each preposition starts a group of words that ends with a noun (under her bed, after school)." Change Learn 2 step 1 to "Look for a small word that starts a group ending in a noun. During starts during the fire drill."

## SHOULD
- [GUM2-7 Teach big + point 1-2] The lesson says a preposition is a word that shows where or when. That is incomplete. The same file uses with, for and to as prepositions in GUM2-6 point 2, and T3's stem uses "played with the dog". Teach also lists through only as a "where" word, but T4 uses it for time ("slept through the noisy thunderstorm"). Fix: add a point: "Some prepositions tell other things, like with, for, about and of." Also add "Some words, like through, can tell where or when."
- [SK-GUM2-7-T4 clue] "Could the thing really be in that place?" pushes a child to reject the correct sentence "We slept through the noisy thunderstorm", because a thunderstorm is not a place. Fix: change the clue to "Picture each sentence. Does the small word make sense with the words after it?" Or replace the thunderstorm distractor with a place, for example "We walked through the tall grass."
- [GUM2-4 Teach point 1 vs SK-GUM2-4-T5, P1] Teach says a sentence that politely tells someone what to do ends with a period. "Could you read this story to me" (T5, keyed ?) also politely asks someone to do something, and could is not in the list of asking words. A child who follows Teach could defend a period. Fix: add to point 1 "But if it starts with can you, could you or will you, it is asking, so it gets a question mark: Could you close the door?" Add could and will to the list of asking words in point 2.
- [GUM2-8 Learn 1 step 1] The step writes the list as "beans, peas and carrots", with no comma before and. Then the answer and step 2 require "beans, peas, and carrots". In a lesson about where list commas go, this shows the child two styles in one worked example. Fix: write step 1 as "beans, peas, and carrots," or name the three things without punctuation: "beans and peas and carrots".
- [GUM2-3 Teach / SK-GUM2-3-P3, T3 clues] Teach never says that small words in a holiday name stay lowercase (Fourth of July). That rule first appears in the P3 clue. Both clues also read almost as answer keys. P3's clue says "The big words ... start with capitals. Small words like of stay small", and the options differ only on exactly that. Fix: add a Teach point: "In a holiday name with more than one word, capitalize the big words; small words like of stay small: Fourth of July, Mother's Day." Shorten the clues to "Is this the name of a holiday? Which words in the name are big words?"
- [SK-GUM2-5-T2] The Test item is keys/keies, which is already worked in Teach (key becomes keys) and in Practice P3 (the same distractor keies, explained in its why). That makes the test too close to practice. Fix: use a vowel+y word not seen before, for example "My grandpa tells stories about his old {0}." with the options jockeys/jockies/jockeyes. Or use "valleys" and drop it from T5.
- [SK-GUM2-9-T3, T4] T3 tests catches and T4 tests studies. Both are examples in the Teach points, and studies is also in the Teach example. Practice P3 (fixes) and P1 (cries) also reuse Teach words. That makes the test too close to the lesson. Fix: in T3 use a new ch/sh/x verb, for example "The puppy scratches at the door." In T4 use a new consonant+y verb, for example "My cousin copies the spelling words."
- [SK-GUM2-1-P2, T3 clues] The clues say "Look for the helping word" and "Which helping word shows...". Teach never uses or defines "helping word". Fix: in Teach point 3, say "Put the helping word will before the verb". Or change the clues to "Look for the word will."

## NIT
- [GUM2-2 Teach big + words] The big idea says a determiner "tells which one or whose", but a and an do not tell which one. Fix: "tells which one, how many, or whose", or "a small word that comes before a noun, like a, an, the, this, those or my."
- [GUM2-2 Teach] The official row names "possessive and demonstrative adjectives". The lesson never uses those terms, only "pointing" and "owning" words. Consider adding them in `words` so parents can match the lesson to the standard.
- [GUM2-2 vs GUM2-6] my, your, our and their are called determiners in GUM2-2 and "possessive pronouns" in GUM2-6. Both terms appear on the GUM chart, but a child taking both lessons may be confused. Fix: in GUM2-6 point 3, add "(these are the same owning words from the determiners lesson)."
- [GUM2-3 Teach point 4] It says "aunt" does not get a capital, but point 1 just showed "Aunt Rosa". Fix: "...teacher and aunt do not get capitals unless they come right before a name."
- [GUM2-3 Teach example] "drove us to Savannah to see the ocean": the city of Savannah is on the river, and the beach is at Tybee Island, about 18 miles east. Fix: "to see the big ships on the river."
- [§6.6 participle after a being verb] These grammar items have one: "is closed" (SK-GUM2-3-P1 option 1, SK-GUM2-3-T1 option 2), "was born" (SK-GUM2-3-P1 option 2, SK-GUM2-3-T1 option 4, GUM2-8 Learn 2), and "were stacked" (SK-GUM2-5-T5 option 2). None of these items targets verbs, so the risk is low. Swap them if the rule is applied literally, for example "The trays sat by the lunch line."
- [GUM2-6 Teach point 3] The stand-alone list "Mine, yours, ours and theirs" leaves out his and hers. Add "hers" at least.
- [SK-GUM2-8-T5] It reuses the Teach example date "May 4, 2026". Use a different date.
