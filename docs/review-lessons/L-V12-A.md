## SHOULD
- [SK-V1a-P2] The clue "Find a sentence that starts with the word habitat" fits only one target (p1:2:2, "A habitat is..."). It works as a surface match that locates the answer, not as a strategy. Fix: "Look for helper words such as is a or is the that come right after the word habitat and tell what it is."
- [3.L.V.1.a p1, paragraph 3, used by SK-V1a-P5 "shallow"] "The water is so low that it barely covers the bird's long legs." The water "barely covers" the heron's long legs, which means it comes up to the top of the legs. That is fairly deep for the bird, so the context clue works against "not deep." Fix: "The water is so low that it only comes up to the bird's knees." Another option is "...that it barely covers the bird's feet."
- [SK-V1b-P1, SK-V1b-P4] The key stands out in both items. In P1, all three distractors (kind, polite, friendly) are words for people, and "ripe" is the only fruit word. In P4, none of the four distractors (helpful, brave, polite, early) is a food or taste word. A child can find the key by category alone, without reading the sentence. Fix: P1 should use food-plausible distractors that do not fit "looked," such as "crunchy," "sour" and "salty". P4 should replace two distractors with taste words that do not fit a good pie, such as "bitter" and "stale".
- [SK-V1b-T3] The `why` says "One exact word takes the place of four plain words," but "come out of their eggs" has five words. Fix: change it to "five plain words."
- [SK-V2b-P1] Drummer is a weak example of "-er added to an action word." To a third grader, drum is a thing (a noun), and the clue asks "Is the base word an action or a describing word?", so a child who answers "neither" is stuck. The `why` has to fall back on "(to drum)." Fix: test a word whose base word is clearly a verb, such as juggler or helper. Juggler is already used in P3, so the item could use "marcher," or "drummer" could be replaced with "player" in the passage.
- [SK-V2b-T3 Part B] Two of the four choices, "-ly" and "-er," are not in the word brightness at all, so the choice comes down to "-ness" or "bright." Fix: use choices that are all parts of the word or plausible clue words, such as "-ness", "bright", "bri-" and "the sun". Another fix is to quote the sentence and offer "brightness," "showed," "bright" and "real" as the clue word.
- [SK-V2c-T5] "Could not help itself" is a common idiom that means "could not stop itself" (could not resist). An adult can read the stem that way, and then "was helpless" changes the meaning. Fix: change the stem to "The tiny kitten was not able to get itself down when it got stuck in the tree." Another option is "had no way to help itself," with the prompt changed to match.
- [SK-V2c-T6] Rows "to build again → rebuild" and "not kind → unkind" repeat the lesson's own Teach and parent examples (rebuild, unkind), and they have the same shape as P3. This makes the test too close to the lesson. Fix: use base words that are not in the lesson, such as "to fill again → refill," "not safe → unsafe," "full of joy → joyful" and "can be broken → breakable."

## NIT

- [SK-V1b-T4] The `why` calls tiptoe "a slow way to move." Tiptoe means moving quietly on your toes. Fix: "Wander and stroll are slow, tiptoe is quiet and careful, and rest means to stop."
- [3.L.V.2.c Teach point 5] The "change y to i before -ly or -ness" rule has common exceptions (shyly, shyness, dryly). Fix: add "most words," as in "In most words that end in a consonant and y, change the y to i."
- [SK-V2a-T1] The distractor "used before school" is not a plausible misreading of re- or -able. Fix: "used one more time only," or "can be used by many people."
