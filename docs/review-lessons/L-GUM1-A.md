## MUST
- [SK-GUM1-1-T5] "The bread smelled good." is a correct sentence, but here *smelled* is a linking verb, not an action. The bread does nothing. The `why` says "The others each tell who or what and an action", which is a false grammar claim. The lesson's only test for a complete sentence is "check for an action word" (Teach point 3 and tip, and this item's clue: "Check each one for an action word"). A child who applies that rule correctly finds no action in *smelled good*, so the item has two answers by the lesson's own rule. Fix: replace that option with an action sentence, for example "The bread cooled on the rack."

## SHOULD
- [1 / Teach] Two Teach points over-simplify in ways that cause trouble. (a) The verb is defined only as "a word that tells an action", so the completeness test fails on any sentence with *is*, *seems*, *smelled* and similar verbs (see 1-T5). (b) "A noun ... tells who or what the sentence is about" treats a noun as if it were the subject. But "The muddy boots by the door" has two nouns and is still a fragment. Fix: say "Most verbs tell an action", and use "naming part" for the subject ("The naming part has a noun that tells who or what the sentence is about"). Also keep every item verb an action verb.
- [SK-GUM1-2-T5] The distractor "The farmer sold a dozen eggs." breaks the rule the lesson teaches: Teach point 3 says "A, an, one and each go with one thing", and the clue says to check the noun after the clue word. A child who follows the rule may flag *a dozen eggs*. An adult can still defend only one key, but the lesson sets this trap, and the `why` never explains it. Fix: change it to "The farmer sold ten eggs.", or add to the `why` that *a dozen* means twelve, so *eggs* is correct.
- [SK-GUM1-4-P4] Clue "Which word starts the sentence?" The only answer to that question is the key *on*, so Help step 1 makes the item a lookup. (8-P1 "Which word tells what kind of kitten?" is close to this too.) Fix: "Think about the two capital-letter rules. Which word breaks one of them?"
- [SK-GUM1-9-T3] The `why` says "A proper noun starts with a capital letter, and the other letters are small." That is not true as a general rule (DeKalb County, McIntosh County, LeBron). Fix: make the claim only about this word: "Augusta is written with a capital A and small letters after it."
- [SK-GUM1-9-P5] The key stands out. *Elm Street* is the only proper noun among the choices, and *Our* is not a noun at all, so the words "that names a place" in the prompt do no work. Fix: add a proper-noun distractor that names a person. Stem: "Our dog Max chased a cat down Elm Street." Choices: Elm Street, Max, dog, cat.

## NIT
- [SK-GUM1-4-T4] "Which word should be a capital letter?" A word is not a letter. Match P4: "Which word should begin with a capital letter?"
- [SK-GUM1-2-T3] Option e uses *every* ("Every girls got a sticker."), but *every* is not in Teach point 3's list of clue words for one thing. Add *every* to that list.
- [6 / Teach big] "The ending on a verb tells when the action happens or who does it." The -ing ending, as the lesson uses it after *loves* and *started*, does neither. Suggest: "The ending on a verb has to fit the sentence: -s, -ed or -ing."
