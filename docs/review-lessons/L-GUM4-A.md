## MUST
- [GUM4-5 Teach tip; also SK-GUM4-5-T5 why] The tip says: "Look at the noun right before the blank. Is it a person, a thing, a place or a time? That tells you which word to use." That claim is false. Whether you use where/when or that/which depends on the clause, not only on the noun. Examples: "Atlanta is the city **that** hosted the Olympics" and "the park **that** has a slide". In both, a place noun comes before the blank and *where* is wrong. The lesson strengthens the error in SK-GUM4-5-T5. Its distractor "The park who has a slide is near us." gets the why "A park and a store are places ... so those words do not fit". A child who follows the tip and that why will "fix" it to "The park where has a slide," which is wrong. No key is wrong, but the rule being taught is. Fix: (1) Rewrite the tip: "Look at the noun right before the blank. A person usually takes who. A place takes where, and a time takes when, if the new part already has its own doer, like 'the park where **we** play'. If the new part needs a doer, like 'the park ___ has a slide', use that or which." (2) Change the T5 distractor to "That is the park who we play soccer." Its fix really is *where*. You could also keep the sentence and change the why to say it needs *that*.

## SHOULD
- [SK-GUM4-3-P2 why] The why says "They also means more than one, but it is used before the action word ... not after it." As a rule, that is not true. In questions, *they* comes after a verb ("Did they eat?" "Are they ready?"). It is also a pronoun-case point, not an antecedent-agreement point. So the distractor *they* tests a different skill from the one this lesson names. Fix: replace the *they* option with *her* or *us*, which are wrong because they do not match *cookies*. Or soften the wording: "They is used for the ones doing the action, as in 'They smelled good.' Here we did the eating, so use them."
- [SK-GUM4-3-P3] The distractor "the science fair" is not in the sentence. A child can drop it without reading. Fix: use a word that is in the sentence, such as "the project" or "science", or a plural noun a careless reader might grab. For example, change the stem to "The girls showed their science projects to the judges." and offer "the judges" as a distractor.
- [SK-GUM4-3-P1, SK-GUM4-3-T2 clues] "Mr. is a title for a man. Is Mr. Okafor one person or more than one?" and "An aunt is a woman. Is Aunt Lucia one person or more than one?" These clues settle both features that decide the answer. Only one option (his / her) is left standing. That is very close to giving the answer away. Fix: use the general strategy clue the lesson already uses elsewhere: "Ask two questions about the noun: one or more than one? A boy or man, a girl or woman, or a thing?"
- [SK-GUM4-4-T5 vs SK-GUM4-4-P5] The test reuses the practice sentence. P5 has "Grandma said, Supper is ready." and T5 has "\"Supper is ready,\" said Grandma." The speaker and the words are the same. SPEC §8 says the test uses different sentences than Practice. Fix: change T5 to new words, for example "{0} said Grandpa." with "\"The garden needs water,\"" and the matching distractors.
- [SK-GUM4-2-T3 vs GUM4-2 Learn 2] Learn 2 is "My cold was bad on Monday, but it was even ___ on Tuesday." → worse. The test key is "My cough is worse today than it was yesterday." That is the same word (worse), the same illness and the same two-days frame, so a child can pass on memory. Fix: change the T3 key to a different irregular or long-word form, for example "Mia's drawing is better than mine." (key) or "This is the most comfortable chair in the house."
- [GUM4-5 Practice/Test coverage] The title, the big idea and the standard's plain wording all name who, which, that, where and when. *Which* and *that* are never a correct answer in any Practice or Test item. They appear only in Learn 2 and as a wrong option ("which" for a person in P1/T1, "which" for a place in P3). So the lesson tests only who/where/when. Fix: key at least one item to *that* or *which*. For example, add a dropdown "I found a shell {0} looks like a star." with options [that, who, where, when].
- [GUM4-7 Teach, point 1 and point 3] The helping-verb list is "has, have, had, will, can, could and should". Point 3 says am/is/are/was/were "are often linking verbs." Then SK-GUM4-7-P4 and SK-GUM4-7-T3 offer is/are/was only as wrong helping verbs. A child will leave thinking be-verbs are never helping verbs, but they are ("is running", "was singing"). I understand that the house rule against a participle after a being verb keeps such examples out of items. Teach should still not imply the opposite. Fix: add to point 3: "They can be helping verbs too, as in 'is running', but in this lesson you will see them as linking verbs." Or record that the omission is deliberate.
