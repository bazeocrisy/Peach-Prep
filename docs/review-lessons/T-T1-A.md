## MUST
- [SK-TT1e-P5] The clue gives away both dropdown answers. It says sentence 7 "is where the search begins" and sentence 9 "is where the long search is over", then says to "choose the time words that fit the beginning and the end." Slot 0 has only one beginning word ("First,"; the other two are end words) and slot 1 has only one end word ("At last,"). After the clue, the child is left with no sequencing decision, and sequencing is the skill being tested. Fix: give the strategy without placing the sentences, for example: "Read sentences 6 to 9 in order. Ask yourself whether each sentence comes near the start of the search or after it is over, then pick the time word that matches."

## SHOULD
- [SK-TT1a-T3 Part B] The prompt asks for a sentence "from the end of the story", but one distractor ("\"You don't have to go,\" her brother Dev said.") comes from paragraph 3. A child can cross it out because of where it sits, without reading for feeling. Fix: swap in a sentence from the end that does not show feeling, such as "Whoosh!", or drop "from the end of" from the prompt.
- [SK-TT1a-T4] The clue ("describes the slide itself, not the park or what Priya does with her hands") rules out three of the five targets and leaves only p2:2:1 and the key p2:2:2. Of those two, only the key actually describes the slide, so this is close to a giveaway. Fix: "Description helps you picture something. Find the sentence that tells how something looks to Priya, not what she does."
- [SK-TT1b-T5] The column "Ending" is never taught. Teach covers only characters, setting, problem and solution, and it says the solution is "how the problem is fixed by the end". The problem also includes "due the next day" (as SK-TT1b-T2 says), so an adult could argue that returning the book on time is the solution. Fix: replace "Ending" with "Character" and add a row "Omar", or add "ending/resolution" to Teach.
- [SK-TT1c-P6 Part B] The distractor "She had wanted that sketchbook all summer." gave me pause. It shows the cost in "even when it costs you", which is half of the Part A key, so a careful adult could argue for it. The `why` explains away "saving money and the pie" but never this choice. Fix: replace it with a sentence that has nothing to do with the cost, for example "He dropped some coins and bills into her hand without looking.", and update the `why`.
- [3.T.T.1.e Test vs Practice] The test mirrors Practice almost item for item, using the same sentence numbers and the same distractor patterns:
  - replace sentence 1 with a strong beginning
  - add dialogue after sentence 6/7
  - replace sentence 10 ("He looked nice." / "It was big.") with a detail; in both, the distractors are "very nice, very good/big", an "about all hamsters/fish" fact, and a "same/kind of" filler
  - order three inserted sentences
  - replace sentence 11 ("The end." / "We went home.") with an ending

  A child can pass by pattern-matching. Fix: change at least two test items, for example ask which sentence should be deleted, which time word fits a new spot, or which detail to add after a different sentence, and vary the distractor types.
- [SK-TT1e-T4] Inserting the three sentences after sentence 9 puts "Then we gently let it go back into the lake." in front of sentence 10, which describes the fish (and in T3 is replaced with "as long as my arm, with shiny silver scales"). The finished draft then describes the fish after it has been released. Fix: change the prompt to "after sentence 10", or replace the third step with one that does not release the fish (for example, "Then Aunt Rosa clapped and cheered for me.").

## NIT
- [SK-TT1a-T5] "an easy way out of the slide" reads oddly. Fix: "an easy way out of riding the slide".
- [3.T.T.1.b teach.big] "Every story has the same four parts" overstates. Fix: "Most stories have four parts".
- [SK-TT1d-T4] In "The main character gets back something that was lost", the stone was Grandfather's, not Pip's, so "gets back" is loose. Fix: "The main character finds something that was lost."
- [SK-TT1e-P4] "Bree pointed at the pantry door" could arguably come before "We tiptoed into the kitchen" if the door can be seen from the living room. The key is still the best order, and one swap earns 1 point. Fix (optional): "Inside the kitchen, Bree pointed at the pantry door, which was open just a crack."
- [3.T.T.1.d Test vs Practice] Test items 1, 2, 3 and 5 follow the Practice stems almost word for word ("How are the settings of the two stories different?" appears in both). The passages are different, so this is acceptable, but varying one stem would help.
- [SK-TT1b-T1] The distractor "the librarian's helper" is not in the story, so a child can rule it out without reading closely. The story only has three people, so this is minor. Fix (optional): add a passing classmate or neighbor to the passage and use that name.
