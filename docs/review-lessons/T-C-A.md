## MUST
- [SK-TC2a-P5] The clue points to the wrong place. It says "Compare paragraph 1 with the last sentence. What does the narrator admit at the end?" The last sentence of "The Yard Sale" is Dev's line, "Thanks for letting me help, Sis." The narrator's admission ("I guess talking to people helps") is the second-to-last sentence. A child who follows the clue reads Dev's words and finds no admission. Fix: "Compare paragraph 1 with paragraph 5. What does the narrator admit at the end?"

## SHOULD
- [SK-TC2a-P1] The clue nearly gives the answer away. "Who says 'my little brother, Dev'?" leaves only one choice that is a sibling ("Dev's big sister"). Fix: "Look for I and my in paragraph 1. Then look at what Dev calls the narrator at the end."
- [SK-TC2a-T3] A careful adult could defend the distractor "The narrator yawns while Grandpa smiles at a bird." Grandpa smiling at the heron is itself a detail that shows the morning another way, which is the skill being tested. The key is stronger, but the sentence this choice draws on sets the two views side by side. Fix: remove Grandpa from the distractor, for example "The narrator yawns while waiting for a fish to bite."
- [SK-TC1b-T4 vs SK-TC1b-P4] The Test item is almost the same item as the Practice item: a bold word, a glossary entry, "why is it bold / where is the meaning." Only the word changes (lodge to chrysalis). This tests memory, not mastery. Fix: replace T4 with a different feature task, for example which heading to read to learn what a caterpillar eats, or how a "Box:" fact differs from the paragraphs.
- [3.T.C.1.c practice and test] Several distractors are too obviously wrong to tempt a careless reader:
  - P3: "jar of peanut butter", "state of Georgia" and "farm machine" as labels for a plant diagram.
  - P5: "a crowd cheering at a ball game".
  - T3: "a school bell ringing".
  - T4: "Write all the words in very small letters" and "Add a long list of science words with no pictures."
  Fix: use on-topic but wrong-part choices, such as a "stem" label, "peanut shell" (not on Slide 2), a tractor driving down a road, frogs splashing into a pond, or "a fact about where peepers live in winter".
- [3.T.C.2.c Teach and SK-TC2c-P1] The standard asks for the context in which a text *was written*. Both passages are boxed as made-up historical fiction, so they were really written today. P1 still asks "When was this letter written?" with "in March 1928" as the key. Inside the story that is right, but it blurs written vs. set, which is the core idea of this standard. Fix: reword P1 to "When did Ella write this letter?" Add one Teach point: "Historical fiction is written today but set in the past; a real letter or diary was written at the time it shows." The Teach example and the two Learn examples are presented as real documents, which is good.
- [3.T.C.1.b Teach] The standard covers features in "print, digital, auditory, spoken, and visual modes." Teach covers only print features (headings, bold, text box, caption, diagram). Fix: add one point, for example "On a screen, links, buttons and videos are features too. A link takes you to more facts; a video or sound shows or tells what words cannot."

## NIT

- [3.T.C.1.a Teach point 2] It names "please" and "should" as persuasion cues. The informational Test page (p2) itself says "Please get there ten minutes early." Consider adding "Directions can use please too; ask what the whole text is mainly for."
- [SK-TC1a-T1] The key, "to tell new players what they need to know," echoes the passage sentence "This page tells you what you need to know." The same sentence is the T2 Part B key, which makes T1 a match-the-words item. Consider "to help new players get ready for practice."
- [3.T.C.2.c Teach example] "January 29, 2014 ... School is closed again!" The storm hit on January 28, while school was in session. I am not certain an earlier closure makes "again" true. Drop "again" (or the exact date) to be safe. The "snow is rare in Atlanta" point is fine.
- [3.T.C.2.a Teach point 1] "Look for I, me, my and we" can mislead in third-person stories that have dialogue (for example, Tasha could say "I hate rain"). Consider adding "outside of quotation marks."
- [SK-TC1c-T4] The distractor "Take out the picture so there is more room for words" assumes Amara's plan has a picture, but the plan is words only. Consider "Take out the heading 'Look for the X.'"
- [SK-TC2c-T3 / T5] Both items test the gray, black-and-white picture detail. Consider swapping one T5 row for "Only a few channels to choose from."
