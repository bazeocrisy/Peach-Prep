## MUST
- [GUM1-1 Teach, points 1 and 3; parent note; SK-GUM1-1-T5] Teach point 3 says that if a group of words "has no noun telling who or what ... the thought is not complete." That is false. A pronoun subject makes a complete sentence ("She ran."). The lesson contradicts itself twice. The parent note's model sentence, "We went to the park.", has no noun subject. SK-GUM1-1-T5 keys "We ate two slices." as complete. A child who applies the taught rule exactly would mark "We ate two slices." as NOT complete, which gives that item two answers for a rule-follower. Fix: in point 1 say the who-or-what part is "a noun, like Maria or the dog, or a word like I, we, she or they". In point 3 replace "no noun telling who or what" with "nothing telling who or what". The `noun` entry in `words` can stay.

## SHOULD
- [GUM1-1 Teach, point 2 and `words` "verb"] This defines a verb only as "an action word." It also says a complete sentence needs a word telling "what they do." Under that rule a child will judge "The sky is blue." or "My dog is happy." to be incomplete. No item uses a linking verb, so no key is wrong. The rule is still misleading, and GUM1-7 ("action verbs") implies that other kinds of verbs exist. Fix: change point 2 to "A verb is often an action word ... Some verbs, like is and was, tell what something is like." Or limit the claim: "Most sentences have an action word."
- [GUM1-2 Teach, point 3; SK-GUM1-2-T3] The clue-word list for one thing is "a, an, one and each". The test distractor "Every girls got a sticker." depends on knowing that every means one, and Teach never says so. Fix: add "every" to the list in point 3, or change the distractor to "Each girls got a sticker."
- [SK-GUM1-2-T3 / SK-GUM1-3-T5] In GUM1-3-T5, two distractors ("Where does your dog sleep?" and "Why does your dog bark?") do not ask about eating at all. They drop out without any thought about question words, so only "When does your dog eat?" competes with the key. Fix: keep the verb eat in all four: "Where does your dog eat?" and "Why does your dog eat so fast?". (Check that neither one asks about the kind of food.)
- [GUM1-6 Learn 2, step 2] "After words like start, stop, keep and love, we use the -ing form" is stated as a rule. "Started to laugh" and "loves to play" are equally correct, and "stop to talk" means something different from "stop talking". Teach point 4 hedges correctly ("You often use them"), but this step does not. Fix: "After words like start, keep and love, we can use the -ing form."
- [GUM1-6 Teach, scope] The row is "Form and use verbs with -ing, -ed, or -s." The lesson teaches -ing only after love/start/stop/keep. It never mentions the most common grade-3 use, after am/is/are/was/were ("Ben is kicking the ball"). I understand that the house rule (no participle after a being verb in grammar items) shaped this. Still, Teach leaves the child with an incomplete picture of where -ing verbs appear. Fix (lead's call): add one Teach point and no items: "You also use -ing after am, is, are, was and were: Ben is kicking the ball." Or record that the omission was deliberate.
- [GUM1-9 Practice/Test, alignment] Three of the 10 items (SK-GUM1-9-P3, SK-GUM1-9-T3, SK-GUM1-9-T5) test only capitalization of a proper noun ("augusta / AUGUSTA", "florida", "tuesday", "max"). That is the separate row 3.L.GC.1:R:capitalize-proper-nouns. T3 and T5 make up 2 of the test's 7 points and never ask the child to tell common from proper. Fix: replace SK-GUM1-9-T3 with a "use" item, for example a dropdown: "We visited my aunt in a big {0}." with options [city, City, Augusta] keyed to city, or a sentence where the child picks the proper noun that could replace a common one. Keep at most one capitalization item.

## NIT
- [GUM1-6 Teach, point 1] "Add -s when one person or thing does the action now" — the simple present usually means something that happens regularly ("every day"), not right now. The example and the items use "every day / each night". Fix: "does the action now or again and again."
- [SK-GUM1-6-P2 clue] "The sentence says the sun comes up, which tells about now." The stronger clue is "Every morning." Fix: "Look at the first words. Does this happen once, or every morning?"
- [GUM1-5 Teach, point 2] The rule is right for this lesson's words. Words ending in z can double the z (quiz → quizzes), and ch that sounds like /k/ adds only -s (stomachs). No item uses either, so this is only a note for the parent if the child asks.
- [SK-GUM1-7-P3 why] "Orange describes a color" should be "Orange is a color word that describes things".
- [GUM1-9 Learn 2, step 3] "Give each word in the name a capital letter" is not true of names with small words (Statue of Liberty, Fourth of July). Fix: "Give each important word in the name a capital letter."
- [GUM1-3 Teach] "How asks about the way something happens" leaves out how many, how much and how old, which children use constantly. Optional: add "How can also ask how many or how much."
