## MUST
- [3.T.T.2.a / passage p2, paragraph 3, sentence 4] "Only one kind of armadillo can roll up into a ball." This is false. Both three-banded armadillo species (genus *Tolypeutes*: the southern and the Brazilian three-banded armadillo) can roll into a ball. It is "one kind" only if "kind" means genus, and an 8-year-old will read it as one animal. The sentence is also quoted as a Part B distractor in SK-TT2a-T2, and it is the basis for the T1 distractor ("why most armadillos cannot roll up…"). Fix: change it to "Only a few kinds of armadillo can roll up into a ball." and change the next sentence to "Those kinds do not live in Georgia." Then update the T2 Part B distractor quote to match the new wording. T2's `why` ("the armadillos in Georgia cannot roll into a ball") stays true.

## SHOULD
- [3.T.T.2.a / Teach point 1] The main-idea example given is "Bees work together to keep their hive alive." That is almost exactly the key to SK-TT2a-P1 ("…each have a job, and they work together") and SK-TT2a-P2 ("…by working together, the bees keep the whole hive alive"). Teach gives away the Practice answers. Fix: use a different topic in the Teach example, such as "sharks" with "Sharks have bodies built for hunting in the ocean."
- [SK-TT2a-P5 Part B] The prompt says "Which two sentences from paragraph 4…", but two of the five choices ("Young workers stay inside the hive." and "Every hive has one queen.") are not from paragraph 4. That is confusing, and those two choices can be ruled out without reading. Fix: replace them with paragraph-4 sentences that do not best support Part A, such as "Older workers fly outside to look for flowers." Or change the prompt to "from the article".
- [SK-TT2a-P2 vs SK-TT2a-T5] Both are "choose the sentence that states the main idea of the whole article." Both use the same target pattern (1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 2:1, 3:1), and both keys are p:1:3. A child who learns "third sentence of paragraph 1" passes T5 without reading. T5 also repeats T1, which already tests the whole-article main idea. Fix: give T5 a different target set and key position, or make T5 ask for a paragraph's main idea or a supporting detail.
- [SK-TT2b-P3 Part B] The key quote, "As a result, many birds learn to leave monarchs alone.", repeats the Part A key word for word, so Part B tests nothing. Fix: make the Part B key "Instead, it stays in the caterpillar's body and makes it taste bad." It shows the link in the chain. Move the "As a result…" sentence out of the choices.
- [SK-TT2b-P6] The prompt asks what the caterpillar does "first" after it hatches. The clue says to "look for a signal word that tells what comes first", and the answer sentence starts with "First,". The clue all but names the sentence, and the item is trivial. Fix: change the clue to "Find the paragraph where the caterpillar hatches. Read its sentences in order." Or ask instead what the caterpillar does right after eating its eggshell (key p1:2:3).
- [SK-TT2b-T2] The clue says "read the sentence just before it". That sentence is "High in the sky, the air is cooler.", which is the key almost word for word. It is shown only in the post-test review, so this is not MUST, but it is still the answer and not a strategy. Fix: change the clue to "Find where the article tells what happens to water vapor high in the sky. What change makes it turn into drops?"
- [SK-TT2c-P4] The two keys are the only choices with the word "energy". The child can match the keyword without integrating anything. Fix: replace one distractor with a plausible one that is not about energy, such as "Then they fly south to Mexico and Central America." Or rephrase the prompt so it does not say "energy" (for example, "how hummingbirds get ready to fly a long way").
- [SK-TT2c-T4] This item asks only where Alligator Moms says the eggs stay warm, which is retrieval from one text. It does not test integrating two texts (3.T.T.2.c). T1 already uses the same fact. Fix: make it a two-text item. For example, ask for the sentence from Alligator Moms that shows a different way warmth helps alligators than the sun in Built for the Water, with targets drawn from both passages. Or swap it for a "how are the two passages alike" item.

## NIT
- [3.T.T.2.a / scope] The standard begins "Describe techniques used to present and design expository texts…". The lesson teaches only main idea and key details. That is defensible, since text features have their own standard, but Teach point 2 could say that the title and first and last paragraphs are where authors place the main idea on purpose.
- [3.T.T.2.a / p1, paragraph 4] "A honeybee has five eyes." (true) sits right after "Older workers fly outside to look for flowers," so it reads as if the eyes help find flowers. That weakens it as the planted irrelevant detail in P4 and P5. Fix: move it to the end of paragraph 4.
- [SK-TT2a-P5 Part A key] "Older worker bees gather food and turn it into honey": only nectar becomes honey, not pollen. Fix: "Older worker bees gather food from flowers and make honey for the hive."
- [3.T.T.2.b / p1, paragraph 5] "A green case called a chrysalis forms around its body": the chrysalis is the pupa's own new skin, not a separate case. It is acceptable for grade 3, but "its new skin hardens into a green case called a chrysalis" is more accurate.
- [3.T.T.2.c / Learn 1] Text 2 never says where the moon's light comes from. It only says "sunny side". "Both texts say" is a stretch. Fix: ask "How do both texts help you know the moon's light comes from the sun?"
- [3.T.T.2.c / Learn 2] "Georgia grows so many peaches that it is called the Peach State" can make a child think Georgia grows the most peaches. Today California grows more. Fix: "Georgia is famous for its peaches and is called the Peach State."
- [SK-TT2c-P2 row 1] "how a hummingbird hovers": Tiny Flyers says it can hover, not how. Fix: "that a hummingbird can hover".
- [3.T.T.2.d / scope] The standard lists "illustrations". No lesson content covers them. That is acceptable since the site shows no images, but one Teach point could mention that a picture or diagram can help explain a fact.
- [SK-TT2d-T4] The sentence numbers show that the three keys are 10, 11 and 12, right after sentence 9, while the distractors are 4, 5 and 7. That makes the item easier than its 2-point DOK 3 label. Consider lowering it to DOK 2, or accept it as is. Do not add sentence 6 as a distractor, since it is about finding food at night and would invite a second defensible answer.
