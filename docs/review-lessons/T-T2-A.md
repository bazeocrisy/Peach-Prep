## MUST
- [3.T.T.2.b / passage p2 "Where Does Rain Come From?", paragraph 2; affects SK-TT2b-T4] The paragraph reads "Water vapor is invisible, so you cannot see it rise into the air. That is why a puddle dries up on a sunny afternoon." Read literally, "That" points back to the sentence just before it, so the passage says a puddle dries up *because water vapor is invisible*. That cause is false, and this is a cause-and-effect lesson. T4's `why` ("Paragraph 2 says this is why a puddle dries up") leans on this sentence. Fix: reorder so the causal sentence follows the evaporation sentence: "The sun's heat makes some of the water change into a gas called water vapor. That is why a puddle dries up on a sunny afternoon. Water vapor is invisible, so you cannot see it rise into the air." (No target or quote refs point into p2 paragraph 2, so nothing else needs to change.)
- [SK-TT2d-P4] Distractor C, "Peanuts are not really nuts, and they grow on plants with yellow flowers.", can be defended as a conclusion. It restates ideas from both body paragraphs (paragraph 2: yellow flowers; paragraph 3: not really nuts). The key only sums up paragraph 2 (how peanuts grow). Grade 3 lessons teach that a conclusion "reminds the reader of the topic or the most important idea", and C does that. The `why` dismisses it as "only repeats facts", but restating is what this lesson says a conclusion does. Fix: make C a plain new middle fact with no summing-up quality, e.g. "Peanut plants need about four months of warm weather to grow." Then update the `why` to say C adds a new fact instead of wrapping up.

## SHOULD

The prompt alone eliminates them, and the prompt is false about its own choices. - [3.T.T.2.a / passage p2, paragraph 3] "Only one kind of armadillo can roll up into a ball." There are two species of three-banded armadillo (Brazilian and southern), and both can roll into a ball. Popular sources say "only the three-banded armadillo", which covers both. As written, the sentence is not quite accurate. Fix: "Only the three-banded armadillo can roll up into a ball. It does not live in Georgia." (Also fix the T2 `why` wording to match if needed. No refs change.)
- [SK-TT2b-P3] The Part A key, "Many birds learn to leave monarchs alone.", is word for word the Part B key, "As a result, many birds learn to leave monarchs alone." Part B becomes a string match and tests nothing. Fix: reword Part A, e.g. "Birds are less likely to eat monarchs." Or make Part B ask for the cause sentence ("Instead, it stays in the caterpillar's body and makes it taste bad.").
- [SK-TT2b-P6, SK-TT2b-P2, SK-TT2b-T2 clues] These clues point straight at the answer sentence. P6: the prompt asks what happens "first", and the clue says to find "a signal word that tells what comes first". Only one sentence starts with "First,". P2: the clue says "Find the word because in paragraph 1", and there is only one "because", which sits inside the answer. T2: the clue says to read "the sentence just before" the vapor-to-drops sentence. That sentence is "High in the sky, the air is cooler.", which is the key almost verbatim. Fix: give the strategy without the locator. P6: "Find the paragraph where the caterpillar hatches and read it in order." P2: "The question asks why. Look for a signal word that points to a cause." T2: "Find where the article tells about water vapor turning into drops. What change happens to the vapor there?"
- [SK-TT2b-P4 slot 0] "The caterpillar eats a lot, because it grows very fast." is grammatical and biologically sensible (a fast-growing animal needs a lot of food). Without a text anchor, an adult could defend "because". Fix: change the prompt to "Use the article. Choose the signal word that best completes each sentence." Or swap "because" in slot 0 for "although".
- [SK-TT2c-P4] The two keys are the only choices that contain the word "energy", which is the word in the prompt. A child can answer by word matching. Fix: make one distractor an energy-flavored sentence from the passages that is not about using or storing energy. Or replace one key with "That is why a hummingbird must eat many times every day." and keep a distractor that mentions energy, e.g. a reworded "Their wings beat so fast that they look like a blur."
- [SK-TT2c-T4] This item only asks the child to find one sentence in Alligator Moms, so it does not test integrating two texts (the standard is "Integrate and explain information from two texts"). The clue mentions Built for the Water, but the task does not need it. Fix: reframe it, e.g. "Built for the Water says adult alligators warm up in the sun. Choose the sentence from Alligator Moms that tells a different way alligator eggs get warmth." Or replace it with a two-text hottext that has one target in each passage.
- [SK-TT2d-T1] The premise "Kenji's draft has no introduction" conflicts with Teach. A careful child who learned Teach would be confused. Fix: "Kenji wants to add an opening sentence that tells the reader what his whole draft will be about. Which sentence should he add before sentence 1?"
- [SK-TT2d-P2 clue] "The next sentence should tell what they are, using a definition." Only one choice contains a definition ("legumes, which are plants that grow seeds in pods"), so the clue identifies the key. Fix: "Sentence 7 says what peanuts are not.
