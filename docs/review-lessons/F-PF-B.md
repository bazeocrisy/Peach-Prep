## MUST
- [3.F.F.1.a / teach.example] The line "could: the l is silent" is true, but the note under it says "The rest of each word follows the rules you know. You only have to remember the tricky part." That is false for could. The ou in could says the oo in book, which is not a sound a grade-3 child knows for ou (out, soup). The tricky part is oul, not just the l. Fix: change the line to "could: the oul says the oo in book, and the l is silent", or swap could for a word whose only irregular part is one letter or letter team (for example "two: the w is silent").
- [SK-FF1a-P3] The clue gives away slot 2. It says "The other has an o that you do not hear," and people is the only one of people / peple / peeple that has an o. Slot 1 is close to a giveaway too: "the same ending as tough" points straight at the only option ending in ough. Fix: make the clue about strategy, for example: "Picture each word in a book you have read. Both words have a tricky part that is not spelled the way it sounds."

## SHOULD
- [3.F.P.4.a / alignment] The standard says "Decode **and encode**." Every Learn example and item in this lesson is decoding (which sound do the letters make). Nothing asks the child to spell. Fix: add at least one encoding item, such as a dropdown "The baker sold a loaf of {0}." with bread / bred / breed, or "Choose the spelling: the s sound in ___ity" with c / s / k.
- [3.F.P.4.b / alignment] Same gap. The standard says "Decode and encode ... words of all syllable types," but every item splits, orders or labels syllables, and none asks the child to spell a multisyllabic word. Fix: add one encoding item, such as a dropdown choosing the correct spelling of a word built from dictated syllables (napkin / napkon / nappkin), or one using the consonant-le pattern (tabel / table / tabble).
- [3.F.P.4.b / learn 1, basket] Step 3 says both syllables are closed "so both vowels are short." The answer then says "you read it BAS-kit." A short e would give "BAS-ket," so the answer does not match the step it follows, and a careful child will notice. Fix: write "you read it BAS-ket", or drop the respelling and keep "bas / ket. Both syllables are closed."
- [SK-FP4a-P4, SK-FP4a-T4] The third dropdown option, "the z in zoo" or "the z in zip," is too obviously wrong. No reader would think g says z, so each slot is really a 2-option choice. Fix: use a plausible third sound, for example "the k in kite" (the hard-c/hard-g confusion) or "the s in sun" (the soft-c/soft-g confusion).
- [3.F.F.1.a / test] The test reuses words from Learn and Practice, so it checks memory rather than mastery (SPEC §8):
  - T3 tests friend/freind (Learn 1 and P4) and laugh (P4).
  - T5's key misspelling is "anser," which is the same misspelling in P4, where the why spells out the fix.
  - T4 uses the same though / through / thought set as Learn 2.

  Fix: test new tricky words. For example, T3 could use "beautiful / bootiful" and "eight / ate", and T5 could misspell "favorite" or "busy".
- [3.F.F.1.a / alignment] The standard is about reading sight words, both regularly and irregularly spelled, in isolation and in context, with accuracy and automaticity. Eight of the ten items ask the child to pick the correct spelling, which tests encoding and recognizing the right visual form, not reading. Regularly spelled sight words are never taught or tested, and the Teach big idea says sight words are the ones that "do not follow the usual sound rules." Fix:
  - In Teach, add that some sight words follow the rules and are learned by heart only because they are so common (for example, "and" and "went").
  - Turn at least one item into a reading-in-context item, like T4: read a sentence and pick the word that fits or the meaning.
  - In the parent note, say that the speed part (automaticity) is best checked by flash-reading at home.
- [SK-FF1a-P2] The clue says "the tricky part is spelled with two letters." Among said / sed / sayed, that leaves only said, because sed has one vowel letter and sayed has three. That is close to a giveaway. Fix: "This word sounds like it has the e from bed, but it is not spelled with e. Picture it in a book."
