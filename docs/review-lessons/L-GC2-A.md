## MUST
- [3.L.GC.2.c / teach.points 3-4] The agreement rules are false for forms of *be* with I and you. Point 3 says "Use is, was, has and does for one", which leads to "I is" and "you is". Point 4 says "With I and you, use the verb with no s", which leads to "I were" and "you are/were" but rules out the correct "I was" (it ends in s). A child who follows these rules on "I (was / were) late" picks "I were". Fix: limit point 3 to "one person or thing (he, she, it, Dad, the dog)" and add to point 4: "With I, use am and was: I am, I was. With you, use are and were: you are, you were."

## SHOULD
- [3.L.GC.2.c / teach.example.note] "More than one puppy chew, with no s." The phrase "more than one puppy" takes a singular verb in standard English ("More than one puppy chews"), so the note models a wrong sentence. Fix: "Two puppies chew, with no s."
- [SK-GC2c-T5] The row "The box of crayons" (matched to is) is the exact subject of Teach point 5's model sentence, "The box of crayons is on the table." The test then checks memory, not the skill. Fix: use a new subject that also has a phrase in the middle, such as "The jar of pickles" or "The pile of leaves".
- [SK-GC2d-T1] The distractor "melted" is a past participle after a being verb ("The ice ... was melted"). That breaks SPEC §6.6, and an adult could argue that partly melted ice is also slippery. Fix: replace it with a plain adjective that does not explain the rail, such as "gray" or "thick".
- [SK-GC2d-T2] This is too easy and too close to P2. The stem already says "reach the fire fast", and all three distractors mean "slow" (slowly, lazily, sleepily), so quickly is the only choice that fits. It is the same item as P2 (turtle / slowly vs quickly) with the meaning flipped. Fix: take "fast" out of the stem and give a context clue instead (e.g., "The alarm rang, and the firefighters ____ climbed onto the truck"). Make one distractor a plausible near-miss, such as "carefully" or "proudly".
- [SK-GC2a-T5] The clue "Two whole thoughts need a comma before the joining word" picks out the key on its own. Only one choice has a comma directly before a joining word, so the child can find it by scanning for ", so" without judging the sentence. Fix: make the clue a strategy, such as "Read each fix. Does it still have both whole thoughts? Are they joined in a way you learned?"
- [SK-GC2d-T5, SK-GC2d-P4] The key stands out on length and content alone. In T5 the key is the only long, specific sentence, and every distractor is short and generic. P4 has the same pattern. A child can pick the odd one out without weighing describing words. Fix: give at least one distractor equal length and one exact word mixed with a plain one (e.g., "A huge elephant did something nice by the fence."). The child then has to judge exactness, not length.

## NIT
