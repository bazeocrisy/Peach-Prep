## MUST
- [SK-V3d-P5] The clue gives away the answer. "The second part is spelled like the word cause" makes the item a search for the only option that contains the string "cause" (becuase, becaus and beacuse do not), so the child does not have to check the spelling at all. Fix: "Say the word slowly in parts: be-cause. Do you hear a smaller word you already know how to spell? Find the spelling that has all its letters in the right order."
- [SK-V3b-P1] The clue gives away the answer. "Which one is a number?" tells the child that the answer is the number word, so a child who knows that "two" is a number can answer without reading the sentence or thinking about meaning. Fix: "All three words sound the same. Read the sentence. Is the missing word telling where, telling 'also,' or telling how many chairs? Then match that to a spelling."

## SHOULD
- [SK-V3d-T3 / 3.L.V.3.d Teach] The Test depends on a rule that is never taught: when one word runs out of letters, the shorter word comes first (plan, plane, planet). Teach point 1 only says to keep comparing letters, and no Learn or Practice item uses a prefix pair. Fix: add a sentence to Teach point 1 ("If one word runs out of letters first, like plan and plane, the shorter word comes first"), and add a prefix pair to SK-V3d-P2 or a Learn example.
- [SK-V3a-P2, SK-V3b-P2, SK-V3c-P3] Each ebsr clue sends the child straight to the Part B key sentence ("Read the very next sentence"; "Kofi says two things about the fish, one right after the other"; "Read the sentence right before overjoyed"). This does not quote the answer, but Part B becomes a lookup. Fix: point to the paragraph and the kind of clue instead (for example, for SK-V3a-P2: "Look in paragraph 3 for a detail that tells how many buyers came").
- [SK-V3a-P5] The clue maps three signal words to three of the four columns and says the leftover row is the synonym clue. That solves the match for the child. Fix: "Look for signal words you learned in Teach. Which kind of clue does each signal word go with?" and do not name the pairings.
- [SK-V3e-P2] The clue "Which verb shows a quick, sudden move?" repeats the definition of darted that the why gives ("To dart is to move suddenly and quickly"), so a child only has to recognize the definition. Fix: "Sentence 5 tells how fast the fish moved. Which verb matches that speed?"
- [SK-V3e-T1] The key stands out. All three distractors (gentle, quiet, soft) mean calm and contradict "big storm," so the item tests opposites rather than precise word choice. Fix: use distractors that are exact words that do not fit wind in a storm, such as "chilly," "damp" and "gentle," or "sticky," "chilly" and "gentle."
- [SK-V3e-P5] The distractors tiny and shallow contradict the sentence ("went all the way up to the ceiling"), so the item tests context, not shades of meaning or precision. Fix: use options that are all "big" words but differ in exactness or fit, such as "enormous," "wide" and "fat," with "wide" and "fat" ruled out because the detail is about height.
- [SK-V3c-P4 slot 2] boiling and freezing are extremes that "just right for a bath" rules out at once, so this slot does not test shades of meaning. Fix: use a nearer shade, such as options "warm / cool / steaming," and change the stem detail so that cool is clearly ruled out (Mia only added "a little" cold water).
