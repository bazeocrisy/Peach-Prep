# Review habits (A)
File reviewed: js/data/habits.js (53 entries, K-12.P.EICC.1.a through K-12.P.CP.2.e)
MUST: 3  SHOULD: 13

Method note: I compared every entry's `looks`, `tryit` and `ask` against the official `text` of its code in js/data/standards.js. All 53 practice records exist there with `tested: "embedded"`, and habits.js has exactly one entry for each, with none missing or extra. I found no brand names. The file uses generic terms like "sticky note", "cereal box" and "library book". Most entries represent their practice faithfully and fit grade 3.

## MUST
- [K-12.P.EICC.2.e] Unsafe at home. The tryit "Read a set of directions or a label together to solve a real problem at home" invites an 8-year-old to work from product labels. At home those are often medicine or cleaning-product labels. Fix: "Read the rules of a board game or the directions for putting a toy together, and use them to settle a real question, like 'Who goes first?' or 'Which piece goes here?'"
- [K-12.P.EICC.2.c] Unsafe as worded. "Read a simple recipe or craft directions together and have your child follow the steps" puts no limit on stove, oven, knife or hot-glue use, and the child does the steps. Fix: "Read a no-cook recipe (like a sandwich, trail mix or fruit cup) or simple paper-craft directions together, and have your child follow the steps while you watch."
- [K-12.P.ST.1.c] The entry misses the core of the practice. The standard says how *context* shapes the author's decisions and the audience's responses. The `looks` text and the first tryit only cover author choices in general ("how the story would change if it were different"), which belongs to AC.1. Only the second tryit is about context. Fix (looks): "Your child thinks about how the time, place and people an author was writing for shaped the author's choices, and how readers in different situations might react differently." Fix (tryit 1): "Look at a story or poem from long ago and find one word or detail the author chose because of when it was written, like 'icebox' or 'wagon', then talk about what an author today would write instead." Fix (ask): "What was happening when this was written, and how did that change what the author wrote?"

## SHOULD
- [K-12.P.AC.1.a] This is a poor fit for grade 3. The tryit uses "a short opinion piece or letter to the editor", and newspaper opinion pages often cover adult or political topics. Tryit 1 also only names elements and never covers the "effect on audience" half of the standard. Fix: "Read a kid's book review or a short opinion paragraph you write together, and find the opinion and the reasons." For tryit 1: "After a story, name the character, setting, problem and solution together, then talk about which one made you want to keep reading."
- [K-12.P.CP.2.b] This cannot be done at home in 15 minutes because it needs a younger cousin and a grandparent. Fix: "Have your child explain what they learned about a topic twice, once as if talking to a kindergartner and once to a grown-up (you can play both), choosing a fitting way for each, like a drawing for the little one and a short talk for the grown-up."
- [K-12.P.AC.3.d] This takes too long. "A comic strip or illustrated mini book" usually runs past 15 minutes. Fix: "Have your child make a 4-box comic, with words and pictures, that teaches a younger child one thing they know how to do."
- [K-12.P.AC.2.d] This takes too long. A "mini poster" with a title, headings and labeled pictures runs past 15 minutes. Fix: "On half a sheet of paper, have your child make a fact card about a topic with a title, two headings and one labeled drawing."
- [K-12.P.AC.3.b] Tryit 2 is vague because nobody is told who writes the sentences. Fix: "Write 6 short sentences about rain on scraps of paper, 3 that sound like a story or poem ('The rain tapped on my window like a drum') and 3 that sound like a report ('Rain forms when water drops in clouds get heavy'), and have your child sort them into 'story' and 'report' piles."
- [K-12.P.AC.2.c] Tryit 2 is vague and too big. It never says which paragraph to use, and rewriting a whole paragraph is a lot for this age. Fix: "Write 3 sentences that all start with 'I' (for example, 'I went to the park. I saw a dog. I threw a ball.') and have your child rewrite them so each one starts a different way."
- [K-12.P.EICC.4.c] Tryit 2 is a garbled sentence that is hard to act on. Fix: "After reading a book, ask your child, 'What does this book make you want to write about?' and jot down two ideas together."
- [K-12.P.EICC.4.d] The "link ideas to the plan" part of the practice is missing. The tryits only pick the strongest ideas and never place them in the plan. Fix (tryit 1): "Have your child list 5 facts or details, circle the 3 strongest, and write each circled one in the box of their plan where it belongs (beginning, middle or end)."
- [K-12.P.EICC.3.c] The "gaps" half of the practice is missing. The entry covers surprises and contradictions but not noticing what the text left out or what the child still doesn't know. Fix (add to tryit 1): "...after reading, talk about what was new and one thing you still wonder about that the text didn't answer." Fix (ask): "What does this remind you of, what surprised you, and what do you still want to know?"
- [K-12.P.EICC.2.d] Tryit 2 is vague and has an unsuitable source. "A short article or ad" gives no guidance, and online ads may not suit children. Fix: "Read the back of a kids' cereal or toy box together and decide whether each claim, like 'the most fun ever!', is a fact you can check or just an opinion."
- [K-12.P.CP.2.e] This is vague. "Part of a famous speech" gives a parent no idea where to start. Fix: "Read a few lines of a famous speech made for young listeners or often taught in grade 3, such as the closing lines of Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.'s 'I Have a Dream', talk about what they mean, and have your child memorize one or two sentences."
- [K-12.P.EICC.2.e] The `looks` example "asking for a later bedtime with good reasons" may make this practice a nightly argument for many families. Fix: "...and writes to convince someone, like suggesting a family movie night or a weekend outing, with good reasons."
- [K-12.P.ST.1.a] Tryit 1 says "look up what life was like there and then" without saying the adult should lead any online search. Fix: "Before reading a story set long ago or far away, look at a library book or search online together to learn what life was like there and then."
